# Integration facts and design

Read this before touching sign-in, playback or the build. Update it when a fact is disproven or a technical choice is made. Facts are dated because YouTube changes.

## Verified facts

- **Toolchain:** Rust 1.98 (ZapFast and Spotifast declare `rust-version = "1.98"`), CMake and a C compiler for the build; at runtime `mpv`, `yt-dlp`, `deno` (yt-dlp's runtime for YouTube's JS challenges) and `secret-tool` (libsecret).
- **Chromium cookie decryption on Linux** (checked 2026-09-30 with Brave Origin 153 and Google Chrome, both using `--password-store=gnome-libsecret`): password = `secret-tool lookup application <brave|chrome|chromium>` with the trailing newline stripped. Key = PBKDF2-HMAC-SHA1(password, `saltysalt`, 1 iteration, 16 bytes); AES-128-CBC with an IV of 16 spaces over the bytes after the `v11` prefix; then PKCS#7 unpadding. From cookie DB schema version 24 the plaintext starts with SHA-256(`host_key`) (32 bytes); strip it. `Local State`'s `os_crypt.portal.prev_init_success = false` means the portal key provider is not in use. Copy the DB before reading; the browser holds it open.
- **yt-dlp's own browser import doesn't help:** `--cookies-from-browser brave:<profile>` decrypted 0 cookies ("cannot decrypt v11 cookies: no key found"), and `brave+gnomekeyring` needs the Python `secretstorage` module, which a system yt-dlp may lack. So ytfast decrypts the cookies itself and gives yt-dlp a Netscape cookie file (mode 0600, a fresh copy per run, because yt-dlp rewrites the files it's given).
- **Exported cookies go stale:** YouTube rotates session cookies, and an exported `cookies.txt` is soon rejected ("cookies are no longer valid… rotated in the browser"). Re-read the live store at launch and on auth failure.
- **Premium formats:** with valid cookies yt-dlp offers `774` (webm/Opus ~271 kbps) and `141` (m4a/AAC ~258 kbps). Without them the best is `251` (Opus ~134–160 kbps).
- **InnerTube with SAPISIDHASH works:** `browse FEmusic_home` as `WEB_REMIX` `1.20260923.01.00` with the cookies and `Authorization: SAPISIDHASH <ts>_<sha1("<ts> <SAPISID> https://music.youtube.com")>` returns `logged_in=1` in about a second.
- **Stream resolution is slow:** `yt-dlp` with cookies resolves itag 774 in 4–10 s per track depending on the CPU (deno solving YouTube's JS challenges dominates; a warm in-process `yt_dlp` still takes ~6 s). **Don't force `player_client=web_music`:** it was faster on 2026-09-30, but on 2026-10-01 it needed a GVS PO token ("Missing required Data Sync ID") and yielded only a 360p video stream. yt-dlp's default clients still return 774.
- **The iOS client's direct URLs don't play:** the InnerTube `player` endpoint as `IOS` (no cookies) answers in ~0.12 s with direct URLs up to 251, and a 1 KB range fetch succeeds, but mpv fails on them after the first bytes.
- **accesskit and broken accessibility buses:** some desktops advertise an AT-SPI bus that refuses connections. accesskit_unix's D-Bus thread then panics on an `unwrap`. With `panic = "abort"` that killed the app at launch, so the release profile unwinds; only that thread ends.
- **Resolve time is yt-dlp's floor** (Dell OptiPlex i5-7500, 2026-10-01): ~3.8–4.9 s per song whether cold, warm in-process (`yt_dlp.YoutubeDL` reused: ~3.8 s) or batched (3 URLs in one run: 12.5 s). Running three resolves at once takes ~4.9 s wall in total, so throughput scales with parallel runs. `player_skip=webpage[,configs]` saves ~1 s but loses every Premium format. The InnerTube `player` endpoint as `IOS` now returns direct URLs that answer 403 at once (PO token enforced), and `ANDROID_VR` answers `LOGIN_REQUIRED`: there is no direct fast path.
- **Loudness:** the `WEB_REMIX` `player` response carries `playerConfig.audioConfig` with `loudnessDb`, `perceptualLoudnessDb` and `trackAbsoluteLoudnessLkfs` (e.g. −10.86 LKFS for "Get Lucky"), in ~0.16 s. On 2026-10-01 five songs had `perceptualLoudnessDb` = `trackAbsoluteLoudnessLkfs` (−7.55 to −11.71) and `loudnessDb` = that minus `loudnessTargetLkfs` (−7).
- **mpv 0.41 (Arch, 2026-10-01):** `volume-gain` exists (dB, −150…150) and works as a `loadfile` per-file option: it applies when that entry starts and reverts when it ends, including the gapless move to an appended entry; a live `set_property volume-gain` during an entry also reverts at its end. `af` takes `@ytfast-eq:lavfi=[volume@pre=volume=-6.0dB,equalizer@b0=f=31:t=o:w=1:g=6.0,…]`; `af-command ytfast-eq g 3.5 equalizer@b0` (and `volume -3dB volume@pre`) changes the running graph without rebuilding it, but `get_property af` keeps showing the string last set. `af-command` on `lavfi-equalizer` (no graph) fails.
- **Like state:** the `WEB_REMIX` `next` response has `playerOverlays…likeButtonRenderer.likeStatus` (`LIKE`/`DISLIKE`/`INDIFFERENT`) for the requested song; its `likeEndpoint` is the `like/like` endpoint with `target.videoId`.
- **Timed lyrics:** `browse` with the lyrics browse id (`MPLY…`, from `next`) as client `ANDROID_MUSIC` `7.21.50`, **without** cookies or SAPISIDHASH (with them: HTTP 400), returns `timedLyricsData`: lines with `lyricLine` and `cueRange.startTimeMilliseconds`/`endTimeMilliseconds` (109 lines for "Get Lucky"). LRCLIB (`https://lrclib.net/api/get|search`, no key) has `syncedLyrics` in LRC form, matched by track name, artist and duration.
- **Most replayed:** the `WEB` client's `next` on `www.youtube.com` (anonymous, ~0.9 s) has `frameworkUpdates…macroMarkersListEntity.markersList` with `markerType: MARKER_TYPE_HEATMAP` and 100 markers (`startMillis`, `durationMillis`, `intensityScoreNormalized`) for a music track.
- **History and Home moods:** `browse FEmusic_history` returns shelves "Today", "Yesterday", "This week"… (200 rows). Home's header `chipCloudRenderer` holds the mood chips (Energize, Feel good, Workout, Relax, Focus, Party, Commute, Sad, Romance, Sleep, and Podcasts).
- **Account writes** (`WEB_REMIX` with cookies and SAPISIDHASH, probed 2026-10-01 in self-undoing pairs; each change confirmed by refetching): `like/like`, `like/dislike` and `like/removelike` with `{"target": {"videoId"}}` rate a song (a fresh `next` shows the new `likeStatus` at once; `removelike` answers `{}`). The same endpoints with `{"target": {"playlistId"}}` save an album or playlist to the library and remove it; an album is saved by its audio playlist id (`OLAK5uy_…`), which the album page's header gives. `subscription/subscribe` and `subscription/unsubscribe` take `{"channelIds": [id]}` with the id from the artist header's `subscribeButtonRenderer.channelId` (a `UC…` id different from the artist's browse id). `playlist/create` takes `title`, `description`, `privacyStatus` (`PRIVATE`) and optional `videoIds` and returns `playlistId`. `browse/edit_playlist` takes `playlistId` and `actions` and answers `status: STATUS_SUCCEEDED` (HTTP 200 also for a refusal: `STATUS_FAILED` with a toast message): `ACTION_ADD_VIDEO` (`addedVideoId`; the answer's `playlistEditResults[].playlistEditVideoAddedResultData` gives each new entry's `setVideoId`), `ACTION_REMOVE_VIDEO` (`setVideoId`, `removedVideoId`), `ACTION_MOVE_VIDEO_BEFORE` (`setVideoId`, `movedSetVideoIdSuccessor`; without a successor the entry moves to the end), `ACTION_SET_PLAYLIST_NAME` (`playlistName`), `ACTION_SET_PLAYLIST_DESCRIPTION` (`playlistDescription`; `""` clears it). `playlist/delete` takes `playlistId`. Unknown ids answer HTTP 404.
- **Duplicates:** `ACTION_ADD_VIDEO` without `dedupeOption` refuses a song already in the playlist (`STATUS_FAILED`, "This track is already in the playlist"); with `dedupeOption: DEDUPE_OPTION_SKIP` it adds a second copy (the dedupe check is what is skipped). ytfast sends no option and reports the duplicate.
- **Account state on pages** (2026-10-01): song rows carry `menu.menuRenderer.topLevelButtons[].likeButtonRenderer.likeStatus` and `playlistItemData.playlistSetVideoId` (playlist and album rows). An album's or other people's playlist's header (`musicResponsiveHeaderRenderer.buttons`) has a `toggleButtonRenderer` (`BOOKMARK_BORDER`) whose `isToggled` is the library state and whose `defaultServiceEndpoint.likeEndpoint.target.playlistId` is the id to save. The account's own playlists have `musicEditablePlaylistDetailHeaderRenderer` (with `playlistId` and `editHeader.musicPlaylistEditHeaderRenderer`), and their Library cards' menus hold a `playlistEditorEndpoint`. Artist headers (`musicImmersiveHeaderRenderer.subscriptionButton.subscribeButtonRenderer`) have `subscribed` and `channelId`.
- **Account changes show with a delay:** a new playlist is listed in Library after ~1.3 s; playlist edits show on the playlist page within 0.2–1.5 s; a subscription shows on the artist page and in `FEmusic_library_corpus_artists` after 1–3 s. Likes and library saves showed at once.

## Design

- **Metadata:** ytfast's own InnerTube client (`src/innertube.rs`) and a renderer-family parser (`src/parse.rs`), one path for every surface. rustypipe was not used (it has no Home feed, and a second session layer isn't needed).
- **Sign-in (`src/auth.rs`):** every Chromium-family profile with a cookie store, most recently used first, or the one chosen in Settings (`~/.config/ytfast/settings.json`, `src/settings.rs`). The account is confirmed with an account-menu request before the UI says "signed in".
- **Streams (`src/resolver.rs`):** yt-dlp only (`-f 774/141/251/140/250/249/139`, a per-run 0600 copy of the cookie file). Resolves run in parallel at two priorities: playback (the current song, then the next) has two slots of its own and never waits behind speculation; speculation (`PrepareMany` with a page's first songs on screen, `Prepare` when the pointer rests on a song, the song two ahead in the queue) has two more, runs under `nice -n 10`, and keeps a most-likely-first backlog of 8. A song asked for twice shares one run; a playback run nobody waits for any more (the click moved on) is killed. Results are cached until 10 minutes before URL expiry, in memory and in `$XDG_RUNTIME_DIR/ytfast/streams.json` (0600), so a relaunch starts at once; entries resolved without cookies don't count once signed in. Each run logs its time and how long it waited for a slot.
- **Audio (`src/mpv.rs`, `src/backend/`):** an `mpv --idle --no-video --no-config` subprocess over JSON IPC. The current track loads with `replace`; the next track, once resolved, is `append`ed, and the move to playlist position 1 is detected and position 0 removed, so mpv's playlist never holds more than two entries. Per-file `loadfile` options carry the user agent (mpv's `%N%` length quoting: they contain commas), the loudness gain (`volume-gain`) and a start time. Repeat-one uses `loop-file=inf`. A stream error retries once through yt-dlp, then skips with a readable error.
- **Loudness levelling (`src/backend/sound.rs`):** at each track start (and for the next track while it resolves) the `player` response gives the song's LKFS; its gain is −14 LKFS minus that, at most +3 dB, as the entry's `volume-gain`. The same response's tracking URL reports the play to history. On by default; `Settings.normalize`.
- **Equalizer (`src/equalizer.rs`):** ten octave bands (31 Hz–16 kHz, ±12 dB) with a headroom preamp, as one labelled lavfi graph in `af`; Flat or off removes it. Band edits go to the running graph with `af-command`, and `af` is rewritten once edits stop (600 ms) so a graph mpv rebuilds has the same values; then `Settings.equalizer` is saved.
- **Queue edits (`src/backend/queue.rs`):** play order with entry ids, plus list order for unshuffle. Play next inserts right after the current song; Add to queue after the additions waiting there, before the rest of the list; shuffle keeps the additions in order after the current song. Whenever the song after the current one changes, the entry appended in mpv is dropped and the new next one appended.
- **Session (`src/backend/resume.rs`):** `~/.cache/ytfast/session.json` holds the queue (play order, list order, additions), the current song and position, volume, shuffle, repeat and autoplay; written atomically on pause, track and queue changes, seeks and exit (`Backend` drop), and every 10 s while playing. A launch restores it paused, resolves the current song at once, and Play starts at the saved position.
- **Sleep timer:** after N minutes or at the end of the song; the volume fades over the last 8 s, then playback pauses (end of song: the next song waits at its start) and the volume comes back.
- **Queue invariant:** `loadfile replace` empties mpv's playlist, so any record of a track appended behind the current one must be cleared on every `replace`. As a safety net, mpv going idle while a track is believed queued advances the queue.
- **Stamps on asynchronous results:** the current track's generation, a queue epoch, mpv playlist entry ids and a page request sequence number. Every late answer is checked against them.
- **Offline:** a song that fails twice is skipped only if YouTube is reachable; otherwise playback waits on it and resumes when a reachability check succeeds.
- **History:** after 10 s of play, GET `playbackTracking.videostatsPlaybackUrl.baseUrl&ver=2&c=WEB_REMIX&cpn=<16 random>` with the same auth headers, from the `WEB_REMIX` `player` response (with cookies) fetched when the song started (fetched then if there is none).
- **Theme:** `fastframe_theme::Catalog` with `DesktopThemes { slug: "ytfast", omarchy_template: BASE_TEMPLATE, presets: false }`. It renders Omarchy's current colours itself and watches for changes, so no hook install is needed. `ytfast reload-themes` exists for a hook.
- **Lyrics (`src/lyrics.rs`):** for the playing song, YouTube Music's timed lyrics (`ANDROID_MUSIC` browse of the `MPLY…` id, no session), else LRCLIB's synced lyrics (`/api/get` by title, first artist, album and length; else `/api/search` with the closest length within ±3 s; `User-Agent: ytfast/<version> (<repo>)`), else YouTube Music's plain lyrics, else LRCLIB's plain ones. Asked for as soon as watch-next names the lyrics page, or 3 s into a song without one. The Lyrics tab highlights by the playback position moved on between backend updates.
- **Cover colour (`src/colors.rs`):** Now Playing alone takes colour from the cover. The bytes the cover loader holds are decoded on the backend runtime (`spawn_blocking`), reduced to 48 px and read in OKLab: the dominant hue's mean colour (deep tone) and the most vivid hue (accent); covers with under 6% coloured pixels count as neutral. The wash is a vertical blend in the cover's hue at a lightness set by the theme (dark or light), with 18% of the theme's window mixed in; text, secondary, dim and accent are the theme's (or the cover's accent) moved in lightness to contrast ratios of 7, 4.5, 3 and 4.5 against both ends. Song changes fade over 400 ms. `App::cover_colors` holds the extracted colours for Stage.
- **Recent searches:** the last 20 queries, newest first, in `~/.cache/ytfast/searches.json`, read and written (temporary file, then rename) on the backend runtime.
- **Most replayed (`src/heat.rs`, `src/ui/ridge.rs`):** asked once per song (`Command::Heat`, cached in `App::heat` by video id) with the anonymous `WEB` `next` described above; no session, so nothing about the account is sent. The peak is the hottest marker after the first 5 % of the song (the start always reads hot), widened to the neighbouring markers at 85 % of its heat or more; `Action::JumpToPeak` seeks to that part's start. The ridge is a Catmull-Rom curve through the lightly smoothed markers, drawn as a mesh over the page in a non-interactive area at rest (7 pt) and an interactive one under the pointer (28 pt, seeks on click). Songs without heat show the plain seek line.
- **Derived covers (`src/derived.rs`):** an egui image loader for `ytfast-soft://<dark|light>/<cover url>` (Stage's field: 72 px, three box-blur passes each way, lightness held in the cover wash's band and colour pushed up within the gamut) and `ytfast-paint://<shadow>-<accent>-<highlight>/<cover url>` (theme-painted covers: up to 360 px, lightness stretched between its 2nd and 98th percentiles and mapped shadow → accent → highlight, the accent at its own lightness). It takes the bytes the cover loader holds and works on the backend runtime (`spawn_blocking`, two at a time). The painting key is the palette's, so a theme switch asks for new images while the previous palette's stand in until they are made; the old ones are freed once nothing on screen needs them.
- **Stage (`src/ui/stage.rs`):** replaces the whole window (`App::stage`). Colours are the Now Playing wash's; the field is drawn stretched with linear filtering, so a frame costs a few textured rectangles and cached text. The cover flies to and from Now Playing (or the player bar) through the `stage` site in `motion`. `Stage::frame_times` keeps `stable_dt` while Stage is open (E2E `surfaces`).
- **egui:** egui 0.36 on the crmne fork uses `App::ui(&mut self, ui, frame)` and an optional `App::logic`, not `update`.
- **Account changes (`src/account.rs`, `src/backend/account.rs`, `src/ui/account.rs`):** optimistic. The app marks the new state (likes by video id, library state by playlist id, subscriptions by channel id) or edits the cached page in the frame of the input, then sends `Command::AccountEdit` stamped with an operation number; a refusal rolls the change back with a plain message. 3 s after a success the backend asks the app to refetch the affected pages, and for 15 s the app's mark wins over what fetched pages say (the delays above). The playing song's rating comes from the same `next` request that finds its lyrics.

## Build

```sh
cargo build --release
cargo fmt --all --check
cargo clippy --all-targets --features e2e -- -D warnings
```

A release build from clean takes about 5 minutes on a 4-core desktop CPU and a few GB of memory. On a small machine, cap it, for example `systemd-run --user --wait --pipe -q -p MemoryMax=5G --working-directory=$PWD -- cargo build --release -j 4`. If two different Rust toolchains are installed, make sure one rustc builds everything (`E0514` otherwise); a machine-local `.cargo/config.toml` (gitignored) can pin `build.rustc`.

## E2E runs

`scripts/e2e.sh [journey|recovery|offline|theme|showcase|motion|pages|desktop|account|engine|engine-restore]` builds with the `e2e` feature and runs the real app on the desktop with the real account, network, yt-dlp and mpv. `src/e2e.rs` clicks controls by their accessible names (`ui::named`) with synthetic pointer events through egui's own input pipeline, types, presses keys, drags, takes framebuffer screenshots and records measurements. It writes `artifacts/e2e/<UTC>-<scenario>/` (gitignored: account data).

- The driver points only at controls drawn in the same place in two consecutive frames. egui lays out a new popup or modal once before showing it (a sizing pass), at a different place: on 2026-10-01 the sleep timer's "End of song" sat ~200 px higher in that first frame and the equalizer's preset chips 14–26 px off, so clicks aimed there landed on Up next or between chips.
- Test-only hooks exist in e2e builds only: `sabotaged(video_id)` hands the resolver an unreachable URL, and `offline()` makes the resolver fail and `Client::reachable` report false.
- `recovery` runs with a stand-in `HOME` holding a copy of a browser profile whose session cookies were overwritten (an expired sign-in). Its reconnect step swaps in a symlink to the real profile.
- `offline` points every request at a dead proxy (`HTTPS_PROXY=http://127.0.0.1:9`).
- `theme`, `showcase`, `pages` and `surfaces` switch the Omarchy theme to `YTFAST_E2E_LIGHT_THEME` (default "Snow") and back.
- `pages` and `surfaces` play songs (they enter the account's history like any play) and put their recent searches back as they found them; `surfaces` also turns theme-painted covers off again.
- `account` changes the real account and puts it back: it likes and unlikes an unrated song on Daft Punk's "Discovery" (playing it for under 10 s, so no play reaches history), saves and removes that album, subscribes to and unsubscribes from Daft Punk, and creates, fills, reorders, trims, renames and deletes a playlist "ytfast E2E". It records `account_before`/`account_after` (saved albums, subscriptions, playlists, the song's rating) and fails if they differ or if a precondition doesn't hold (album saved, artist subscribed, a leftover "ytfast E2E").
- `showcase` runs with an empty stand-in `HOME` (the real Omarchy theme linked in), so it is signed out and its screenshots hold no account data. The README's pictures come from it.
- `engine` then `engine-restore`, in that order: `engine` ends with a session and writes what the next launch must show to `~/.cache/ytfast/e2e-engine-expected.json`; `engine-restore` checks it and puts back the volume, equalizer and levelling. In e2e builds the backend records what mpv reports (`volume-gain` per track, `af`, the sleep fade) for these scenarios.
- Never run two scenarios at once: each stops any running ytfast.
